A minimal Bayesian pit-timing experiment. A car has eight laps left on a tyre set with an unknown low or high degradation rate. Staying out provides a noisy signal; pitting commits to a replacement set with known performance.

This is a mechanism demonstration, not evidence of real F1 time savings. Parameters are illustrative. No historical race data is used.

What this experiment actually tests

Both the adaptive and frozen-belief policies update after every observed signal. Only the adaptive policy anticipates those future updates when choosing its current action. The frozen-belief policy and posterior-mean policy coincide here because fixed-schedule costs are linear in degradation.

Only one optional stop remains. After pitting, there are no further decisions, so new-tyre observations have no decision value in this toy problem. Actual lap-time observations, multiple stops, uncertain replacement tyres, traffic and race rules remain future work.

The example button selects the largest gain among initial-action disagreements with 80% signal accuracy. It is an illustrative case, not an out-of-sample estimate.
