EXPERIMENT 07 · CONTROLLED SYNTHETIC STUDY

Uncertain lap times do not tell us how much we can learn.

All three cases start at the same expected excess lap time (2.04 s) and predictive variance (4.6836 s²). What changes is how much comes from an unknown degradation regime versus fresh observation noise.

Where does learning change the decision?

Each row changes pit loss while keeping this case’s other assumptions fixed. Both policies update after real observations; only the adaptive planner anticipates future updates.

Can we identify the hidden degradation?

A separate simulation adds an unknown pace offset that persists across all eight observations. The correct inference model integrates out that offset. This is a recovery diagnostic; the decision planner above still assumes a known baseline.

Brier score measures regime-probability error (lower is better; always predicting 50% scores 0.25). Recovery observes all eight laps, even when a policy would have pitted earlier.

What this establishes

Initial predictive variance alone is insufficient to determine information value. The full predictive distributions differ between cases, and changing regime spread also changes the physical stakes. These are illustrative parameters, not fitted F1 effects. Known-baseline planning gains cannot be transferred to an unknown-baseline setting.

One optional last stop; known replacement tyre performance; independent Gaussian observation noise; correct regime prior. Numerical optimisation is approximate. No real-race strategy benefit or novelty claim is established.
